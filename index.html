Work examples

Each project below demonstrates the five OPWL program learning goals in practice. Start with whichever interests you.

Needs Assessment: LMNOP Aviation
A global aircraft maintenance provider where most service centers were missing productivity targets and fewer than half met invoice accuracy thresholds. Our recommendations targeted the mix of environmental and skill factors driving the gaps, with the goal of improving profitability and customer satisfaction across the network.
Program Evaluation: Tamrack NEO
Tamrack, a 200+ employee cleaning supply company serving hospitals and nursing homes, had revised its new employee orientation for virtual delivery but never formally evaluated it. Our team assessed whether new hires felt welcomed and were prepared to learn their roles. The recommendations target improved new hire readiness, stronger team connections, reduced turnover, and fewer on-the-job errors.
Leadership & Cultural Analysis: Nimbus Commerce
A business unit losing market share and missing revenue goals. Drawing on interviews conducted over six weeks and operating artifacts, this analysis found a cultural overbalance toward short-term revenue at the expense of innovation, specialist depth, and people development. The recommendations target a rebalanced culture that retains technical talent, broadens success measures beyond quarterly revenue, and rebuilds the conditions for long-term profitability.
Instructional Design: Nominal Group Technique
Hale Associates' consultants were spending billable hours facilitating a data-gathering technique that clients could learn to run themselves. We designed instruction to teach clients how to facilitate Nominal Group Technique, freeing up consultant capacity for higher-value performance improvement work.
Onboarding Proposal: St. Luke's Hospital
A hospital system opening three new sites in 24 months needed a scalable onboarding program for physicians, nurses, support staff, and volunteers in a hybrid work environment. Our proposal applied evidence-based adult learning principles to design a longitudinal program that gets new hires ready to perform across roles and locations, with a business case built for a non-L&D executive audience.
E-Learning Video: Herban Dispensary Group
A single-location dispensary expanding to three stores could no longer rely on the owners to onboard every new hire informally. I designed a blended onboarding with self-paced content and in-store performance support aids. This video artifact teaches bud tenders about terpenes so they can make more accurate product recommendations, improving customer satisfaction and retention.

About

My development as a practitioner

After four years at Apple, I knew something was off, but I didn't have the frameworks to name it. Supporting data annotators and QA teams had shown me that organizational friction kills good technical decisions, but I was still defaulting to training as the fix.

The OPWL program changed that. The frameworks I learned gave me a way to see that most performance gaps aren't knowledge problems. Prosci's research showed me that change adoption depends on readiness conditions, not communication volume. Binder's Performance Chain connected individual behavior to organizational results. And Schein's work on culture embedding mechanisms helped me understand why some changes stick and others don't.

The practitioner identity I've built from those frameworks is centered on one idea: people readiness for change. The work I do now, through Ensyte and through this program, starts with diagnosing whether people have what they need to do what's being asked of them. That diagnosis comes before any intervention design, every time.
